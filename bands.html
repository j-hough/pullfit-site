No bands yet? Start anyway

PullFit tracks your hands, not the band, so you can try every exercise without one. Learn the moves, find the ones you enjoy, then add a band when you're ready to make it harder.

What kind of band?

Look for long loop bands, often sold as "pull up bands": a single closed loop about 2 metres (208 cm) long. In PullFit you stand on the band for most exercises and pull against it, so the long loop is what every movement is designed around.

  • Short mini bands (the small loops that go around your legs) are too short for PullFit's exercises.
  • Tube bands with handles can work for some moves, but the exercises are designed for loops.
  • No door anchor needed. Only one exercise, the Pallof Press, has the band fixed at your side: loop it around something sturdy like a heavy table leg, or use a door anchor if your set includes one.

Which resistance for which exercise?

  • Light (about 5 to 35 lb): front and lateral raises, curls, tricep extensions and reverse flys.
  • Medium (about 30 to 60 lb): chest press, rows, shoulder press and wood chops.
  • Heavy (40 lb and up): squats, deadlifts and lunges.

Add your bands in PullFit under Settings → App Settings → Manage Bands. Your fitness test results and personal records are then kept for each band, so you can see your progress as you move up.

Band safety

  • Check your band for nicks, tears or thinning before every workout, and replace it if you find any.
  • Keep the band firmly under your feet, and wear trainers so it can't slip out.
  • Most loop bands are made of natural latex. If you have a latex allergy, choose latex-free bands, like the Flintronic set above.
